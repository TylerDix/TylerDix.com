A billion in $100 bills out-towers the Burj Khalifa (2,717 ft) by a third — and weighs as much as a fully loaded city bus.

The point

When someone has a billion dollars, they don’t have “more than” a millionaire. They have a thousand of them — and you can’t spend it down in 27 years trying.

“The rich” and “the very rich” aren’t neighbors. Mind the gap.

The math & sources
  • Seconds: 1,000,000 s ≈ 11.6 days; 1,000,000,000 s ≈ 31.7 years (÷ 86,400 s/day, 365.25 days/yr).
  • Earning: U.S. real median household income was ~$80,610 in 2023 (U.S. Census Bureau). Rounded to $80,000. $1B ÷ $80,000 = 12,500 years.
  • Spending: $1,000,000,000 ÷ $1,000/day = 1,000,000 days ≈ 2,738 years. At $100,000/day ≈ 27.4 years.
  • Stacks: a U.S. $100 bill is ~0.0043 in (0.109 mm) thick and weighs ~1 g (U.S. Bureau of Engraving & Printing / Federal Reserve). $1B = 10,000,000 bills → ~3,583 ft (1.09 km) tall, ~10,000 kg. Burj Khalifa: 2,717 ft (828 m).
  • Timeline: 1 pixel = $10,000, so the bar runs to 100,000 px at $1B. All positions are exactly to scale — no zoom or compression.

Figures are rounded for clarity; the goal is honest scale, not penny precision.
